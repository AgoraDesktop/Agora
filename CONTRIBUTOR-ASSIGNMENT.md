## Background

A. Assignee maintains **Agora**, an installable desktop environment for Linux and the BSDs
   comprising the Cocoa stack, a window server, a desktop shell and its applications,
   organised as a federation of repositories under the GitHub organisation
   `https://github.com/AgoraDesktop` (the "**Project**"). The Project's own licensing
   arrangements are recorded in `LICENSE`, `LICENSE-CHANGE.md`, `COPYING`,
   `COPYING.LESSER` and `THIRD-PARTY.md`.

B. The Project is currently owned by a single copyright holder. Contributions made by
   anyone else introduce additional copyright owners into the tree, which prevents the
   Project from being relicensed, from adopting different outbound terms per artifact, and
   from defending its copyright as a single plaintiff.

C. You wish to contribute code, documentation or other material to the Project, and the
   Parties intend that the Assignee hold all copyright in that material so that the Project
   has one owner.

D. In exchange, the Assignee grants you back a broad licence to your own contribution
   (clause 5), and you obtain the benefit of having your contribution considered for,
   included in and distributed with the Project.

E. The Parties intend this Agreement to take effect under, and to be governed exclusively by,
   the laws of Canada. It is made in writing and signed by you for the purpose of satisfying
   subsection 13(4) of the *Copyright Act*, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-42, which provides that no
   assignment is valid unless it is in writing signed by the owner of the right.

## Notes for the Assignee (delete before publishing)

1. **Fill in** the date, both parties' addresses, Annex B's entity details, and the notice email
   in clause 14.8; the forum Province is fixed as Ontario. Nothing should be offered for
   signature with ⟨placeholders⟩ still present. Governing law is fixed as the laws of Canada
   throughout — do not reintroduce a foreign-law choice, and do not add back the old
   "mandatory provisions of the law of your habitual residence" carve-out; it would split the
   governing law and undo the point of this revision.
2. **"Solely the laws of Canada" has one practical consequence.** Private-law questions
   (formation, capacity, remedies, enforcement) are, in Canada's bijural system, provincial or
   territorial law, and no contract can be governed by federal statute alone. Clause 14.3
   therefore reads "laws of Canada" but names Ontario for forum, and the copyright claims go
   to the Federal Court, which has concurrent jurisdiction over copyright under s. 20 of the
   *Copyright Act*. If you prefer a single forum, drop the Ontario reference and keep the
   Federal Court for everything, but expect a contract claim to be transferred to a superior
   court, most likely the Ontario Superior Court of Justice. Have counsel confirm the formula
   before first use. Note also that naming Ontario does not adopt Ontario law: Ontario's
   *Electronic Commerce Act, 2000* is relevant only as an example of the e-signature
   legislation that validates the Annex A signature, and the substantive law stays federal.
3. **s. 13(4) is a formality you cannot skip.** An assignment in Canada is void unless it is
   **in writing signed by the owner**. A CLA bot that records "I agree" in a pull-request
   comment is not enough on its own, and neither is a click-through modal. Collect a signed
   Annex A per contributor, store the signed instrument (a private repository — it contains
   personal information), and keep the PR reference with it. Clause 14.9 and Annex A say this
   expressly; do not soften them.
4. **s. 14 reversion cannot be contracted out of.** Where the contributor is the first owner,
   the assignment stops being operative 25 years after their death, the balance reverts to their
   estate, and any agreement by the author disposing of that reversionary interest is **void**.
   Clause 2.5 acknowledges it rather than fighting it, and clause 2.1 no longer claims "all
   reversions". Do not add a clause purporting to assign the reversion: it is void and invites
   an argument that the drafter did not understand the statute. The practical exposure is small
   — everything already released under GPL-3.0-or-later / LGPL-3.0-or-later stays licensed to
   every recipient for the full term (clause 2.5(d)) — but it does close the door, 25 years
   after a contributor's death, on collecting all copyright in one hand. Ask counsel about
   s. 14(2) (collective works) if clean title ever matters commercially.
5. **s. 14.1 moral rights are a waiver, not an assignment.** The Act says moral rights "may not
   be assigned but may be waived in whole or in part" (s. 14.1(2)) and that an assignment does
   not by itself waive them (s. 14.1(3)). Clause 6 is drafted as an express, written, separate
   waiver in favour of the owner, so downstream licensees can invoke it under s. 14.1(4). If
   you ever rewrite clause 6, keep those three hooks — a generic "waives all moral rights to
   the extent permitted by law" sentence is weaker than it looks under Canadian law.
6. **s. 13(3) is the trap for employed contributors.** An employer is the first owner of
   copyright in a work made in the course of employment, absent an agreement to the contrary.
   Annex B exists for that, and clause 8.1 now stops a submission until it is signed. A

7. **Contributors resident outside Canada.** The Agreement is governed by Canadian law, but
   the law of the contributor's own country can still decide whether they owned the right there
   and whether it could be transferred, and whether their local moral rights could be waived.

   and France are the usual problem cases; clause 3.2 keeps the fallback inside the s. 14
   reversion limit so the two provisions cannot be read against each other.
8. **Consideration and cause.** Common law needs consideration; Quebec civil law needs a

   for a token sum, recite one dollar in clause 11 and actually pay it — a recited but unpaid
   sum is worse than none.
